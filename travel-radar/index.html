Travel Radar

A feature for TornTools that learns how long each foreign item stays empty, and tells you when to fly so you land as it restocks.

Runs the branch's radar-core.ts in your browser against Prometheus, starting from what it learned in a recording on 2026-10-28. Times are TCT. A fresh page needs a minute to confirm the shelves, and it has to see an empty item sell out again before it can time it.

What the branch adds

The Travel Table shows what is in stock right now. Shelves abroad refill on each item's own rhythm: plushies stay empty about 15 minutes, flowers about an hour, Xanax about two. For slow sellers it hardly matters when you fly. For items that sell out within minutes of a refill, you need to land as the refill happens, and flights last 24 minutes or more. The radar watches the feed in the background, learns each rhythm, and works backwards from the next restock.

Leave-now notification

For items you watch: fly by this time and you land at the early edge of the predicted restock window. It accounts for your ticket type.

Restocked notification

When a watched item comes back and should still be there when you land. It stays quiet when it would sell out mid-flight.

Travel Radar panel

On the Travel Agency page: every predictable item with its plan, and a bell to watch it. Off by default.

Does the timing hold?

The logic comes from a personal Android app I've used for a month. I ported it to TypeScript and replayed the same 48 hours of YATA history (26–28 September) through both versions. All 15 counts the Kotlin tests print came out identical in the port.

Prometheus also publishes its own nextRestock. For the 8 restocks with one published at least 10 minutes ahead, it was off by a median 91 minutes. The radar doesn't use it.

How it fits TornTools

Off by default
One switch under Travel (settings.pages.travel.radar). It turns on the background learning and the panel. Notifications sit with the other types (settings.notifications.types.travelRadar) and only cover items you watch.
Feed use
One fetchTravelData() call every 5 minutes while it's on and the browser is open, through the user's own service priorities. No new Torn API calls: it reads the existing userdata for status (only pings when you're Okay) and ticket type.
Where the code is
features/travel-radar/radar-core.ts (pure logic, no I/O), its tests, the panel feature, and background/updates/travel-radar.ts, called from timedUpdates like the NPC alerts. Existing files gain 36 lines: settings, the storage key, the switches and the feature registration.
Storage
One key, travelRadar: about 50 KB for the ~230 items the feed lists.
Checks run
288 tests pass (20 new), plus types, Svelte check, Oxlint and Oxfmt. wxt build for Chrome works, and the extension loads in Chromium: the background read the live feed and the panel rendered with no console errors.

What I'd like to know from you

  1. Polling. Is one background read every 5 minutes acceptable for users who turn this on? Reading every minute catches far more of the fast items' restocks: in the app's replay it confirmed about 83% of them, against about 33% at 5 minutes. But it's five times the load on Prometheus and YATA.
  2. Placement. A separate panel, or columns in the Travel Table (next restock, leave by, a bell)?
  3. Shape. Seeds for six items ship in code so it predicts from the first check. Fine, or learn from scratch? And one PR, or the core and background first, then the panel?
